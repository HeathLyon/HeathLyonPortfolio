HEATH LYON // YOLO FALL DETECTION PROJECT BRIEF
================================================

STATUS
Desktop Python research prototype. The portfolio page plays a recorded video;
the browser does not execute the Python model or request a visitor's camera.

PURPOSE
Detect a person, estimate body pose, evaluate fall-like motion over time, render
a privacy-aware skeleton view, log the event, and optionally send an alert.

THE FIVE PROCESSING STEPS
1. CAPTURE
   camera.py opens the selected video source, validates each frame, and releases
   the hardware when the application closes.

2. DETECT
   pose.py runs YOLO pose inference. It returns body keypoints and confidence
   values, then rejects detections that are too incomplete or unstable.

3. COMPARE
   fall_detector.py compares body angle, hip height, vertical movement, and
   posture history. One horizontal frame does not automatically mean a fall.

4. CONFIRM
   Configurable timing thresholds require fall-like evidence to persist. A
   recovery state and cooldown reduce repeated alerts for the same incident.

5. RESPOND
   renderer.py draws the privacy-aware display, alert_logger.py records the
   event, and an optional notification adapter can forward an alert. Secrets
   must remain outside public source code.

WHY FALL AWARENESS MATTERS
These U.S. figures apply to adults age 65 and older:

- More than 14 million older adults, about 1 in 4, report falling each year.
- About 37% of those who fall report an injury needing medical treatment or
  restricting activity for at least one day. CDC estimates about 9 million
  fall injuries annually.
- Falls lead to roughly 3 million emergency-department visits and about
  1 million hospitalizations among older adults each year.
- The age-adjusted older-adult fall death rate increased 21%, from 64.7 deaths
  per 100,000 in 2018 to 78.4 per 100,000 in 2024.
- CDC identifies falls as the most common cause of traumatic brain injury and
  estimates roughly 300,000 hip-fracture hospitalizations caused by falls each
  year.

DANGERS AND CONSEQUENCES
A fall can cause traumatic brain injury, hip fracture, hospitalization, reduced
mobility, loss of independence, or death. Fear after a fall can also reduce
activity; reduced activity may weaken strength and balance and increase future
risk.

PROTOTYPE LIMITS
This project is a research and portfolio demonstration. It is not a medical
device, diagnostic tool, or emergency-response service. Computer vision can
miss events or create false alerts. It should not replace professional care, a
validated monitoring system, or emergency services.

BEFORE A PUBLIC SOURCE RELEASE
